MilliHertzBase.jl

Gravitational-wave layer for the analysis of telemetry of the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA), built on the domain-general StreamingInference.jl: noise models, waveforms and detector response for massive black hole binaries, the products of the LISA Data Challenge (LDC), the pipeline stages that turn them into labelled, whitened records, and the payload of the DeepSpaceTelemetry producer, a simulator of the telemetry downlink of a spacecraft. LISA observes through time-delay interferometry (TDI), combinations of its laser links, time-shifted by the light travel times along the arms, in which the laser frequency noise cancels.

Installation and status

The package requires Julia 1.13 or later and is not registered; its first release is 0.1.0 (tag v0.1.0, 15 October 2026). An environment adds StreamingInference.jl by URL at its release tag (the commit recorded under [sources] in Project.toml), then this package at its tag or following main:

using Pkg
Pkg.add(url = "https://github.com/PaulGoG/StreamingInference.jl", rev = "v0.1.0")
Pkg.add(url = "https://github.com/PaulGoG/MilliHertzBase.jl", rev = "v0.1.0")  # the release
Pkg.add(url = "https://github.com/PaulGoG/MilliHertzBase.jl")                  # main

Channel modes

A TDI record holds the Michelson-type combinations X, Y, Z; the stages work on the noise-orthogonal combinations A, E, T (tdi_to_aet). The mode is set once, under [tdi] channels, and every product records it in its sidecar ([product] channels).

  • "A", the default: the single channel A, under product stems without a suffix.
  • "AE": preprocess_record whitens A and E each by its own power spectral density (PSD) and combines the features of the two channels into as many as the A mode has; the product stems gain _ae. [preprocessing] channel_combination selects the combination: "max" (default) keeps of every feature the value farthest towards a signal — the larger band power and spread, the smaller entropy — so that a source one channel sees is not diluted by the other; "mean" takes the features of the averaged periodogram, the excess power of the network, which is the better statistic only for a source split equally between the channels. label_truth_stream adds the onset of a detector that reads both channels, from the network signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) $\rho_{AE}^2 = \rho_A^2 + \rho_E^2$ (orthogonal noise, Prince et al. 2002, doi:10.1103/PhysRevD.66.122002), as signal_start_index_ae.
  • "AET": no features of the T channel are defined, and the pre-processing refuses the mode; the labelling stage sets the three-channel onset to the AE onset and records that it did, and the payload export writes the column T. The response of T to gravitational waves is strongly suppressed relative to A and E below about 10 mHz.

The signal onset from which alerts are credited is the first window in which the truth stream reaches [ldc] label_snr_threshold, 3 by default. By default (psd_model = "record") the truth is conditioned as the pre-processing stage conditions the observed record (record_file, else the file of the truth stream) — high-passed and whitened by the record's own PSD, the Galactic foreground included — and the SNR of a window is the norm of the whitened signal in it: the signal as the detector sees it, against the noise it faces. With the name of an analytic TDI PSD ("sangria", "spritz", …) the window SNR is the matched-filter SNR of the raw truth against that instrument noise; a threshold of 5 there corresponds to about 3 against the record.

A record with gaps — samples marked NaN, as in the LDC-2b (Spritz) products — is pre-processed stretch by stretch: every stretch between two gaps is high-passed and whitened on its own, the Welch estimate is pooled over the stretches so that no segment spans a gap, and the windows are those of the grid of the record that lie inside a stretch, less the edge margin at both ends of every stretch. The sidecar of such a product lists the stretches (stretches, with gap_samples, under schema 2), and window_indices of StreamingInference.jl gives the record window of every row.

Telemetry payload and replay

The payload of the telemetry producer (export_telemetry_payload) follows the channel mode: the A channel as one column Amplitude, or under "AE" and "AET" the columns A, E (and T), which the producer (DeepSpaceTelemetry 2.6 or later) carries as one payload — one batch, one transfer, one loss for all columns. The delivery of a batch is then common to its channels by construction, and a replay reads them from the run (open_telemetry_run(run_dir; columns = ["A", "E"])).

A mission that carried the A channel alone can be replayed on A and E. StreamingInference's ScheduledRecordRun serves every delivered batch from the TDI record (channel_record), after checking its A column against the payload the mission carried. This assumes that E would have been delivered with A, which holds on LISA, where all TDI combinations are formed on the ground from the same telemetry; the link of such a mission was sized for one channel. mode_events returns the event table with the onset of the channel set a detector reads.

whitening_psd_from_sidecar returns the whitening PSD recorded in the sidecar of a feature product, one PSD per channel for a multichannel product.

Example

An IMRPhenomA signal at a matched-filter SNR of 20 in Gaussian noise of the LISA sensitivity, and the output of the matched filter on that record:

using MilliHertzBase, StreamingInference, Random

fs = 0.2                                     # sampling rate [Hz]
h, _, _ = phenoma_waveform(fs, 2 * 86400.0; total_mass = 2e6, mass_ratio = 2.0)
h = scale_to_snr(h, fs, 20.0; psd = lisa_noise_psd)
matched_filter_snr(h, fs; psd = lisa_noise_psd)   # ≈ 20
record = synthesize_noise(Xoshiro(1), length(h), fs; psd = lisa_noise_psd, f_min = 1e-5) .+ h

# Whitened, the noise has unit variance per sample, so the filter output is
# the SNR plus a Gaussian draw of unit variance
wh = whiten_record(h, fs; psd = lisa_noise_psd)
wd = whiten_record(record, fs; psd = lisa_noise_psd)
sum(wd .* wh) / sqrt(sum(abs2, wh))          # ≈ 20.1
